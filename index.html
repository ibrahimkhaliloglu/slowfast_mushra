How natural does this synthesised speech sound?

These sentences were generated from ultrasound videos of the tongue, without any recorded audio. You will compare several versions of each sentence with the original recording and rate how close each one sounds to it. This is a MUSHRA test (MUltiple Stimuli with Hidden Reference and Anchor, ITU-R BS.1534).

How it works

  • Use headphones in a quiet room. Set a comfortable volume on the first reference and keep it.
  • There are 10 trials, one sentence each, about 20 minutes in total.
  • In each trial, play the Reference (the original recording), then play every sample A–J. You can switch between them while they play and replay them as often as you like: the new one carries on from the same moment of the sentence.
  • The reference and every sample have a waveform lane that shows where you are in the sentence (the samples' lanes show the reference's waveform as a guide). Click a lane to play that sound from that moment, or drag across a part, one word say, to repeat just that part; the same part then repeats on every sample you switch to.
  • A quick way through: first find the hidden reference and the clearly worst sample, then compare the rest. Sort by score puts the samples in the order of your ratings, so you can compare neighbours and adjust. ⇄ Swap (or X) flips between the last two sounds you played, e.g. two samples or a sample and the reference, without stopping: press it as often as you like.
  • Rate each sample from 0 to 100 for overall quality compared with the reference: how natural and how clear it sounds, and how close it is to the reference.
  • One of the samples is the reference itself, hidden. Find it and rate it 100. Each trial needs at least one sample at 100 before you can continue.
